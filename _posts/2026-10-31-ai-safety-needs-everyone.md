Change is a process of creation within which destruction and manipulation are by nature inherently present. For something new to arise, something old must transform. In the case of artificial intelligence, its future capabilities and its development trajectory are difficult to fully understand, however what is virtually certain is that this technology will radically transform the shape of our economies, societies, and industries. In the face of such dramatic change, the stakes are too high to restrict who gets to be a part of the conversation of AI safety. Truly, everyone has something to contribute, and it would benefit humanity to have as many minds facilitating the regulation of this technology as possible.

Creating a sustainable, safe world which responsibly interacts with AI is a difficult endeavor. Getting this right will require a tremendous, highly coordinated and multidisciplinary effort which must pull from the expertise and experiences of researchers, policy makers, professionals across diverse fields, and the broader public. For these groups to be informed and productive, the necessary communication networks must exist. People need to be aware of the problems that must be addressed, they need to understand the relevance of the problem and the context of its implications, and they must understand how they can personally contribute their time and effort towards a solution. Exposure and easy access to useful, actionable information is a major bottleneck to getting more people to work on globally relevant AI safety issues, and developing this infrastructure quickly is of critical relevance to the general wellbeing of humankind.

## Awareness isn't the problem

A study conducted by Oxford University's Reuters Institute suggests that public AI awareness is virtually universal.[^1] AI risks and rhetoric are becoming prevalent in the mainstream media, and in Canada nearly half of professionals regularly encounter AI tools in their work.[^2] General awareness therefore is not the problem. Rather, according to findings from the University of Toronto's Schwartz-Reisman Institute, the problem is that people are not exposed to good information that teaches specific risks and their implications.[^3] A report from the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance reinforces this idea by concluding that technical concepts and research are being poorly articulated to non-technical professionals and to the public. This report strongly argues that better communications infrastructure must be developed to focus on translating AI safety issues and technical research into generally legible, actionable information.[^4]

The world must demand that AI technology be meaningfully governed and that its risks be mitigated. This is not a controversial sentiment. In the US, studies show that people, regardless of political affiliation, overwhelmingly believe that AI must be regulated and that their government is not doing enough to do so.[^5] A majority even believe that the government should be stricter with its treatment of AI even if it were to mean losing an arms race with China.[^6] There clearly exists widespread international concern which can be channeled into the coherent political will and social momentum required to meaningfully implement industry level changes and to pressure the government and the broader international community to seriously regulate artificial intelligence. This energy can best be harnessed through communication channels and productive education.

Relative to the industries which develop the technology, AI safety is new, small, and under-resourced. Its networks are underdeveloped, and it is doing a poor job at integrating its interests and influence with other relevant fields. Those working in AI safety also struggle to effectively share the work they do with the people beyond their limited reach. Consequently, the field is failing to upskill and make use of the many people across the world who could contribute towards giving humanity its best chance of survival and prosperity.[^7] This article then is a direct request to you, the reader, to offer your help in addressing one of the greatest challenges our species has ever been faced with. So, to offer your time and talent to the cause, what can you do?

## What you can do

**If you are a university student**, check to see if your school has a community interested in AI safety. If there is, then get involved. Otherwise, consider starting a club, or running initiatives to share your passion and concerns. There are many great university clubs around the world which dedicate themselves to working on some of the issues described in this article, and they would likely be very happy to give you advice if you reach out. If you would like, you can talk to me. I am the policy lead at the University of Toronto's AI safety club (Toronto AI Safety Initiative).[^8]

**If you are an educator**, consider incorporating AI safety discussions into your classes or work. Regardless of your academic background, discussing how AI is interacting with your field and what you think motivated students can do to work on the problems that you notice would be a valuable use of your time. If you are also an academic, then there is likely fresh, useful work that you can do to apply theory and methods from your field into the study of machine learning systems and how they engage with the world.

**If you are a professional**, investigate how AI safety is currently engaging with issues that relate to your field, and think about whether you could contribute your own expertise to these projects. If you are considering a career change, then there are great organizations like 80,000 Hours which could help walk you through this process.[^9]

**Finally, for anyone, regardless of your background**, the following is some general advice. Firstly, it is important that you stay informed, as doing so is a prerequisite for engaging in useful discussion and for making more direct contributions. To do this, you could read articles on The Frontier Watch, find a reading list of useful sources online, or talk to people who know more than you do. As a democratic citizen, you could contact your elected representatives through a thoughtful email to express your concern on AI related issues. Here, outline what you or others you agree with want your representative to do.[^10] If you are interested in joining or supporting a social movement, you could investigate groups like PauseAI, the Center for AI Safety, or the Future of Life Institute. Additionally, as a final recommendation, if there is some unanswered research question you find interesting, a problem which is not being addressed, or a group which does not exist, then take it upon yourself to do that which others have not. Immerse yourself in the problems you care about, and the world will be a better place for it.
